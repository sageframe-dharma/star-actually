Zvols

definition
depth 2/3 − deeper + surface

A zvol is a slice of the pool that pretends to be a blank disk instead of a folder of files. You hand it to something that wants a whole disk of its own—most often a virtual machine (a computer simulated in software)— and that thing formats and uses it exactly as if it were real hardware.

The confusion it resolves: people go hunting for "the file" that holds their virtual machine's disk and find nothing—because there is no file. The virtual disk is carved straight from the pool. And underneath the pretense it's still ZFS: fingerprint-checked, freezable in time, copyable to another machine, like everything else in the pool.

Where you meet them: Proxmox carves one zvol per VM disk (that's why zfs list on a Proxmox host shows .../vm-103-disk-0 entries where you expected files). The practical grammar: created with zfs create -V 32G tank/vm-disk; snapshots and send/recv work exactly as for datasets—which is how a VM disk gets hourly history and offsite replication with the same machinery as everything else.

Two zvol-specific knobs worth knowing exist: volblocksize is the recordsize question in block-device form—fixed at creation, workload-matched (virtualizers set sane defaults); and thin vs thick (-s for sparse) decides whether the pool promises the space up front or lets you overcommit—overcommitted thin zvols on a filling pool are a classic slow-motion incident.
