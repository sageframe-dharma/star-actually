Sanoid and Syncoid

concept
depth 2/3 − deeper + surface
Snapshots, backups, and actually sleeping at night 6/6 ← prev next →

Configured once, together, in one file, they blur into a single mental object: "the backup system." Actually, they're two separate, thin tools wrapping two ZFS primitives you can run by hand. "Sanoid is a policy-driven snapshot management tool" (its README)—a scheduler over zfs snapshot and zfs destroy: take hourlies/dailies/monthlies, prune on retention. Syncoid "facilitates the asynchronous incremental replication of ZFS filesystems"—an orchestrator over zfs send | zfs recv: find the common snapshot, ship the increment, retry sanely.

Neither adds storage machinery. It's cron-shaped judgment on top of snapshots—which is why understanding snapshots and send/recv is understanding these tools.

The working setup (the practitioner's homelab runs exactly this shape): /etc/sanoid/sanoid.conf assigns each dataset a template—

[tank/services/postgres]
  use_template = production   # hourlies pruned in days; frequent history
[tank/media]
  use_template = archive      # weekly, kept for months; slow history

—and a timer runs sanoid --cron. Separately, a scheduled syncoid --recursive tank/services backup-host:vault/services replicates. Two decisions worth making deliberately rather than by default: retention per dataset (churn-heavy data accrues snapshot rent fast—see Snapshots and Disappearing Space), and replication cadence per dataset (how much history are you willing to lose—that's your real RPO, in systems terms: the age of the newest copy elsewhere when disaster hits).
