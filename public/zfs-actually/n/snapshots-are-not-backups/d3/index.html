Snapshots Are Not Backups

Snapshots, backups, and actually sleeping at night 4/6 ← prev next →

The feeling is earned: hourlies, dailies, monthlies on every dataset—you can recover anything from any point in time. That's what backups do, so surely you have backups. Actually, ask one question of any protection scheme: what failures does it survive? A snapshot lives on the same disks, in the same pool, behind the same controller, in the same building, under the same admin as the data. Pool death, fire, theft, ransomware with root, a fat-fingered zpool destroy—the snapshot dies in every scenario the word "backup" exists for.

Snapshots answer "the data changed wrongly." Backups answer "the place failed." Different questions.

What snapshots are, said positively: the versioning half of a backup strategy—recovery from deletion, bad upgrades, corruption-by-software, your own past self. Instant, free, high-resolution. Most restores you'll ever perform come from a snapshot, because most disasters are small.

The other half is replication: zfs send the snapshots to a different pool on a different machine (syncoid on a schedule does exactly this). Now the failure domains actually separate—and the classic 3-2-1 shape (three copies, two media, one offsite) maps cleanly onto ZFS: live pool + snapshots, replicated pool in the house, one leg somewhere else entirely.

The test that keeps everyone honest: a backup you haven't restored from is a hypothesis. Browse the replica's .zfs/snapshot/, restore one real file, once a quarter.
