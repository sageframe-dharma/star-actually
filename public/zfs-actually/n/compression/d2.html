The inherited belief: squeezing data to save space costs speed—a tax you pay only when the space is worth it. Actually, the method ZFS uses (named LZ4) is so fast that the trade usually runs backward: squeezing the data takes less time than writing and reading the extra unsqueezed bytes would. Compression frequently makes storage faster. It also gives up quickly on data that won't squeeze (photos and video are already compressed), so those cost you almost nothing. Leaving it on everywhere is the official recommendation—"set compression=lz4 on your pools' root datasets... unless you have a reason not to enable it" (OpenZFS docs).

Your decades-old instinct about slow compression was correct when you formed it. The hardware moved; the instinct didn't.
