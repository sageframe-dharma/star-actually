Rollback

procedure
depth 2/3 − deeper + surface

Proxmox-style VM snapshots teach a seductive model: the timeline is a scrubber bar—jump to Tuesday, look around, jump forward again. Actually, zfs rollback is demolition, not navigation: making Tuesday the present destroys everything after Tuesday—the data since, and every later snapshot. Permanently. There is no roll forward, because forward no longer exists.

⚠ DESTRUCTIVE—verify before running. The -r flag doesn't protect later snapshots; it confirms their destruction (rollback to a non-latest snapshot refuses to run without it—the refusal is the safety, the flag disarms it). Semantics per zfs-rollback(8).

The decision procedure, because the destructive verb is usually the wrong one:

  • Want one file from the past? Copy it out of the hidden .zfs/snapshot/<name>/ directory. Nothing is destroyed; this is most restores.
  • Want to inspect or experiment with the past? zfs clone tank/ds@tuesday tank/ds-tuesday—a writable branch sharing blocks, costing nearly nothing. Look around, take what you need, destroy the clone.
  • Certain the present is worthless? That's rollback's one legitimate case: zfs rollback tank/ds@tuesday. Before running it, say out loud what dies: all data since Tuesday, and every snapshot after it (-r to acknowledge). If saying it caused hesitation, take a snapshot of now first—snapshots are free, and future-you may want to inspect the wreckage.
