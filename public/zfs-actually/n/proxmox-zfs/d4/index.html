ZFS Under Proxmox

scenario
depth 3/3 − deeper + surface

This is how most homelabs run ZFS for years without meeting it: Proxmox offered "ZFS" at install, VMs appeared, storage worked. The naive model—VM disks are image files on a filesystem somewhere—sends you hunting for qcow2 files that don't exist. Actually, Proxmox carved each VM disk directly from the pool as a zvol (a block device, not a file), and the mysterious rpool is just the pool the installer made for the OS itself. (Operational source: the practitioner's own Proxmox Docker hosts run exactly this stack.)

Decoding a typical host:

  • zpool status—rpool (the installer's, often a mirror of the boot disks) plus any pool you added. All the vdev/scrub/status reasoning from this site applies verbatim.
  • zfs list—rpool/ROOT/... (the OS), rpool/data/vm-103-disk-0 and friends (zvols, one per VM disk), and on a services host, datasets like tank/services/<name> per deployed service—filesystem face and block-device face of the same pool, side by side.
  • The RAM ledger—the ARC and your VMs share physical RAM, and the give-it-back dance is imperfect on a VM host; this is the one place capping zfs_arc_max is orthodox rather than superstition (see The ARC). Budget it deliberately: RAM = VMs + ARC cap + host slack.
  • Proxmox's own VM snapshots ride the same machinery—but sanoid policies on the zvols and syncoid replication offsite are what turn "snapshots exist" into a strategy (see Sanoid and Syncoid).

The two models reconciled—the point of this node: "datasets for services" and "zvols for VMs" felt like different systems when learned separately. They're one pool wearing two faces per consumer type: containers and services want filesystems (datasets—snapshot boundaries per service); VMs want disks (zvols—block semantics per guest). Policy, retention, replication, scrubs: identical underneath.

Two Proxmox-specific edges worth respecting: double-CoW—a guest running ZFS/btrfs (or qcow2-on-ZFS) on top of a zvol amplifies every write; give guests ext4/XFS unless you have a reason. And thin zvols overcommit the pool—five VMs each promised 100GB on a 300GB pool works until they all mean it; the free-space discipline gets less forgiving when the claimants are machines (see The Free-Space Discipline).
