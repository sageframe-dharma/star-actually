Copy-on-Write

concept
depth 1/4 − deeper + surface
Snapshots, backups, and actually sleeping at night 1/6 ← prev next →

The naive parse of "copy-on-write" is a safety feature—something like journaling, keeping a copy in case a change goes wrong. Actually, copy-on-write means ZFS never overwrites a live block, ever. When you "change" a file, ZFS writes new blocks somewhere fresh, then atomically flips pointers so the new blocks become the truth. The old blocks aren't defended or recovered—they're simply never touched.

The consequence: at every instant, the on-disk state is some complete, consistent moment. A write either fully happened or never happened. There is no torn middle—which is why there's no fsck, and no journal replaying half-finished work.
