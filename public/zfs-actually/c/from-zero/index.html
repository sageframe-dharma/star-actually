4 steps, in order — start anywhere

  1. What a Filesystem Is The absolute ground floor: a disk stores only numbered blocks and has no idea what a file is—the filesystem is the ledger that invents files, names, and folders on top of them.
  2. A Short History of Filesystems Fifty years in one story: each generation of filesystems answers the previous generation's disaster—ending (so far) at the generation ZFS opened, which stopped trusting the disk itself.
  3. The Filesystem Landscape The filesystems you actually meet—ext4, XFS, NTFS, APFS, Btrfs—one honest line each, and the specific combination ZFS offers that the others don't.
  4. What ZFS Is A system for storing data built around one promise: what you put in comes back exactly—or ZFS tells you, loudly, instead of silently handing you something broken.
