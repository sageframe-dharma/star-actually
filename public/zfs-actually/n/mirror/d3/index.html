Mirrors

Build your first pool with intent 4/9 ← prev next →

A mirror vdev is two (or more) disks holding identical copies of every block. Capacity math makes it look like the naive option—you "lose" half your disks. Actually, the capacity you give up buys the three things parity can't sell you: reads served by any member (IOPS scale with disk count), resilvers that are a straight copy from a healthy twin, and vdevs you can grow, split, and rearrange with the least ceremony in ZFS.

Where mirrors are simply the right answer: pools whose workload is random I/O—VMs, databases, container volumes. The pool stripes across vdevs, so six disks as three mirror vdevs gives roughly 3× write IOPS and 6× read IOPS of a single disk; the same six as one RAIDZ2 gives roughly 1× (see RAIDZ for why). This is the whole "mirrors for VMs" folk wisdom, derived.

Operations worth knowing exist: zpool attach converts a lone disk into a mirror (the retroactive fix for a single-disk-vdev regret) or adds a third member to a pair—and resilvering a mirror is reading one healthy disk, not reconstructing from the whole vdev. Growth path: replace both members with bigger disks one resilver at a time, or add another mirror vdev to the pool.
