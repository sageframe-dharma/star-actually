You'll want to call ZFS a filesystem, because that's the word the ecosystem uses. Actually, ZFS is the entire storage stack in one system. The jobs that are normally three separate tools—the filesystem (the ledger that turns blocks into files), the volume manager (the software that gathers many disks into one usable space), and the RAID layer (the software that keeps redundant copies so a dead disk costs nothing)—are merged, on purpose, into one.

Everything else you'll meet—pools, checksums, snapshots, scrubs—falls out of one founding assumption: your hardware is lying to you. Disks return wrong data without error, controllers drop writes, power dies mid-operation. ZFS is what storage looks like when you refuse to trust any of it.
