The Free-Space Discipline

concept
depth 3/3 − deeper + surface
Where the speed actually comes from 5/5 ← prev next →

Intuition says a 95%-full disk is just a disk with less room—capacity and speed are separate ledgers. On ordinary filesystems that's mostly true. Actually, copy-on-write makes free space a working resource: every write—including "overwrites," including deletions—needs fresh space to land. A full pool forces the allocator to hunt for scraps, writes fragment into whatever gaps exist, and the pool slows down in a way that doesn't fully undo when you free space later, because the fragmentation already happened.

Free space isn't headroom. It's the workshop floor CoW does its work on.

Operating numbers, honestly labeled as heuristics: keep general-purpose pools below ~80–85%; performance-sensitive pools (VMs, databases) appreciate more slack. Past ~90% the allocator visibly changes character; at ~100%, deletion itself gets weird—freeing space needs CoW writes too (snapshot destruction is the usual escape because it frees without writing... unless snapshots are exactly what's pinning everything—see Snapshots and Disappearing Space).

Watch two numbers: zpool list for CAP and FRAG. FRAG measures free-space fragmentation (how shredded the remaining room is), not file fragmentation—a high-FRAG, high-CAP pool is one whose future writes are already compromised. The honest fixes are the blunt ones: grow the pool (add a vdev), or want less (retention policy, quotas so one dataset can't eat the floor).

Why this is structural, not a tuning miss: an overwrite-in-place filesystem at 95% mostly rewrites the blocks files already own—its steady state barely needs free space. CoW never rewrites in place, so its demand for fresh, preferably-contiguous space is perpetual; the same distrust of overwrites that buys snapshots-for-free and no-fsck sends the bill here. There's no configuration that removes the bill—only layouts and habits that keep it small.

Neighbors: quotas and reservations are the per-dataset tools that keep one tenant from consuming the shared floor; snapshot retention is the usual silent filler (USEDSNAP grows while everyone stares at USEDDS); and the resilver connection closes the loop—full pools resilver slower, so the free-space discipline is quietly also failure-window discipline.
