Sanoid and Syncoid

concept
depth 1/3 − deeper + surface
Snapshots, backups, and actually sleeping at night 6/6 ← prev next →

A snapshot routine that actually protects you—taken every hour, old ones cleaned out, fresh copies shipped to another machine—is a chore no human keeps up by hand at three in the morning. So most serious ZFS setups run two small helper tools, usually installed together and easily mistaken for one thing called "the backup system." Actually, they're two separate helpers, each automating one job you can do yourself. Sanoid is the snapshot-taker: "a policy-driven snapshot management tool" (its README)—it takes snapshots on a schedule (hourly, daily, monthly) and deletes old ones according to rules you set. Syncoid is the shipper: it "facilitates the asynchronous incremental replication of ZFS filesystems"— it finds the last moment both machines share, and sends over only what's new since.

Neither adds any storage machinery of its own. They're scheduling and judgment layered on top of snapshots and send/receive—which is why understanding those two ideas is understanding these tools.
