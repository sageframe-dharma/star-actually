Build your first pool with intent 2/9 ← prev next →

The naive model—a pool is a big partition—will let you operate for a long time without being true. Actually, a pool is a reservoir: you hand ZFS whole disks, arranged into vdevs, and get back one undifferentiated pot of capacity that everything on top shares. Nothing inside a pool has a fixed size—no partitioning, no pre-allocation, no "resize the volume" ever again.

The dangerous half of the correction: the pool is not where redundancy lives. That's the vdev's job, one layer down—and the distinction is the difference between a safe layout and a time bomb.
