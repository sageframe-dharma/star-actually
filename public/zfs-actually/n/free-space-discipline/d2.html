Where the speed actually comes from 5/5 ← prev next →

Intuition says a nearly-full disk is just a disk with less room—how full it is and how fast it is are separate questions. On ordinary systems that's mostly true. Actually, because ZFS never writes over existing data, every change needs fresh, empty space to land on—even "overwrites," even deletions. A full pool forces ZFS to hunt for scraps of room; new data gets shredded into whatever gaps exist; and the pool slows down in a way that doesn't fully recover when you free space later, because the scattering already happened.

Empty space isn't spare room. It's the workshop floor every change is made on.
