Scrubs

concept
depth 3/3 − deeper + surface

The word sounds like cleaning—you scrub a thing to fix it up. Actually, a scrub is a read-through: ZFS reads every piece of data the pool holds and checks every fingerprint ("a normal scrub examines all data in the specified pools and verifies each block's checksum"—zpool-scrub(8)). Why force that? Because ZFS checks data whenever it's read anyway—but the files nobody has opened in three years never get read. Data can quietly go bad while sitting untouched, and a scrub visits it while a spare copy still exists to fix it with.

The fixing itself only happens where a spare copy lives: "for replicated (mirror, raidz, or draid) devices, ZFS automatically repairs any damage discovered." Scrub a setup with no spare copies and ZFS hands you a list of what went bad—named files, even—and can heal none of it.

The practice: zpool scrub tank, online, background, throttled behind real traffic—expect hours to a day-plus on big HDD pools. Monthly is the homelab convention, usually via a systemd timer or cron a distro already installed (check before adding a second one). Then read the results in zpool status: repaired 0B ... with 0 errors is the sentence you want. A scrub that repairs a few blocks did its job; a device whose CKSUM count rises scrub after scrub is announcing its retirement—that, plus a rising trend, is your early-warning system working.

Why it must be scheduled at all: your hot data self-verifies on every read. The archive nobody has opened in three years verifies never—unless something walks it. The scrub is that something.

Boundaries:

  • vs resilver—same machinery, different scope and occasion: scrub reads everything on schedule; resilver reads what's needed to rebuild a member after replacement. Sequence them—resilver restores redundancy, scrub then certifies the whole pool (and note: starting a replace cancels a running scrub, per zpool-replace(8)).
  • vs fsck—fsck repairs structure after crashes, offline, on a filesystem that can be inconsistent. CoW made that job obsolete. Scrub audits content against physics, online. People reach for "it's like fsck" and it's nearly the opposite: fsck assumes the software failed; scrub assumes the hardware did.
  • The scrub-of-death legend—scrubbing with bad RAM supposedly destroys pools; it doesn't survive inspection (see The ECC Question). Scrub with confidence.
