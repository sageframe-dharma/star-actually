The ECC Question

concept
depth 2/3 − deeper + surface

The claim arrives with total confidence: running ZFS on non-ECC RAM is reckless—a scrub with bad memory will "correct" your good data into garbage, pool-wide. It has the shape of expert knowledge: specific, counterintuitive, scary. Actually, here's Matt Ahrens, co-creator of ZFS: "There's nothing special about ZFS that requires/encourages the use of ECC RAM more so than any other filesystem." ECC is good engineering—for every filesystem equally. ZFS on consumer RAM is still safer than ext4 on the same RAM, because everything ZFS verifies is still being verified.

Why the "scrub of death" fails on inspection: for a scrub to overwrite good data, the repair path has to accept a corrupted reconstruction—and acceptance requires the reconstruction to match its checksum. Random RAM corruption producing data that happens to hash correctly is a hash collision by accident—odds so remote they aren't a scenario, they're a rounding error (the jrs-s.net analysis walks the arithmetic). Bad RAM makes a scrub fail to repair things, loudly; it doesn't convert the repair machinery into a corruption engine. The myth requires the safety system to be the weapon, and the safety system checks its own work.
